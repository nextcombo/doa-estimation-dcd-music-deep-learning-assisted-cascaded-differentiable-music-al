# DOA Estimation: DCD-MUSIC: Deep Learning Assisted Cascaded Differentiable MUSIC Algorithm for Multi-Source Near-Field Localization [with MATLAB Code]

Abstract: Future wireless technologies require precise positioning of multiple users in the near field. The mainstream method involves subspace decomposition of the input covariance, which is achieved by searching for peaks on the MUSIC (Multiple Signal Classification) spectrum. This method is suitable for non-coherent sources with sufficient number of snapshots and calibrated arrays. This paper proposes a deep learning-assisted cascading Differentiable MUSIC (DCD-MUSIC) algorithm, enhancing the near field positioning capability of MUSIC through a dedicated deep neural network (DNN), enabling reliable and interpretable operation. DCD-MUSIC utilizes two trained DNNs to generate an alternative covariance, one for reconstructing source angles and counts and another for calculating the distance MUSIC spectrum. This is facilitated by a novel learning method: firstly, facilitating the partitioning of signal and noise subspaces; secondly, converting MUSIC into a differentiable machine learning model. Results show that DCD-MUSIC can successfully locate multiple coherent near and far field sources.
Introduction: Future wireless systems are expected to adopt large antenna arrays and explore high frequency bands such as millimeter waves and Asia-Pacific Hertz. These technologies combined present two characteristics: firstly, precise positioning becomes crucial; secondly, users often reside in the near field (hereafter referred to as near field).
The near field environment brings possibilities not available in the far field. Far field narrowband sources can only be located via direction of arrival (DoAs), while near field spherical wavefronts can simultaneously recover both DoAs and distance. Algorithm tools for locating multiple near field sources include beamforming, cumulative-based schemes, and subspace methods based on covariance (e.g., 2D MUSIC, multilevel MUSIC, etc.). Subspace methods are attractive due to their resolution not being limited by array geometry and their interpretability and simplicity, but they rely on various modeling assumptions, such as non-coherent sources, calibrated arrays, and a large number of snapshots.
Another method involves leveraging deep learning to learn from data for positioning. Various deep learning networks have been used for such tasks, including multi-layer perceptrons, convolutional neural networks, and attention models. Although black-box architectures rely on large amounts of data training with high-parameterized and unexplainable architectures, deep learning networks can be combined with classical positioning algorithms through model-based deep learning. In the context of far-field arrival direction restoration, deep learning networks combined with subspace methods can generate MUSIC spectra and alternative covariances for arrival direction restoration. While this design can break through limitations such as coherent source applications by using subspace methods, it is only applicable to far-field sources and relies on differentiable arrival direction estimation, which cannot be extended to near-field scenarios.
This paper proposes DCD-MUSIC, a deep learning network-assisted algorithm for near-field positioning based on subspace. It retains the interpretable operations of the subspace near-field positioning locator, especially capable of obtaining effective feature spectra and MUSIC spectra that can be used for identifying the number and location of the source. DCD-MUSIC is based on the ESPRIT-MUSIC cascading, decomposing the process of arrival direction and distance restoration. For arrival direction, by referring to related methods, it uses the differentiability of ESPRIT to train a dedicated deep learning network to calculate an alternative far-field covariance for restoring arrival direction and introduces a novel training loss to encourage this covariance to estimate the number of sources from the feature spectrum. For distance restoration, it proposes a soft-to-hard approximation of MUSIC to train another deep learning network, calculating the covariance for distance restoration through each arrival direction's 1D MUSIC calculation. Experimental results show that DCD-MUSIC can outperform multiple benchmark methods for positioning multiple coherent near-field and far-field sources.
Conclusion: This paper proposes DCD-MUSIC, a data-driven enhanced algorithm for ESPRIT-MUSIC for near-field positioning. DCD-MUSIC employs deep learning networks to generate spectral graphs for identification and positioning of sources. The evaluation results show that DCD-MUSIC achieves precise positioning and performs better than similar deep learning baseline methods.
